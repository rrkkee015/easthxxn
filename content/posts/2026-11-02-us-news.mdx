## 2. Judge Blocks Trump Policies on Fast-Tracking Children's Asylum Cases  


A U.S. judge has stopped some of former President Trump’s policies that tried to speed up asylum cases for children. These policies aimed to quickly decide if children could stay in the U.S. or not. However, critics argued that fast-tracking these cases could harm children by not giving them enough time to explain their situations. The judge said that the policies might not follow the law properly. This is a big win for immigrant rights groups who want fair treatment for children seeking asylum. The decision may force the government to slow down and improve how these cases are handled. It also shows how courts can check government actions on immigration. We will see if the government appeals this decision or changes its policies.

## 3. The Strangest U.S. Job Market: Low Hiring and Low Firing for Three Years  


The U.S. job market is unusual because companies are hiring fewer people, but also firing fewer workers. This "low-hire, low-fire" economy has lasted for three years. Usually, when the economy changes, companies quickly hire or fire workers to adjust. But now, many businesses are keeping their staff steady. This makes it hard for job seekers to find new positions, but also keeps workers' jobs safer. Experts say this could be because of economic uncertainty and changes in how companies work after the pandemic. The strange job market affects the whole economy because it changes how money moves and how people spend. Understanding this trend helps us see what the future of work might look like.

## 4. Two Indian American Teens Skip India’s Engineering Race and Raise $1.3M in Silicon Valley  


Two 18-year-old Indian American entrepreneurs chose not to compete in India’s tough engineering exams. Instead, they moved to Silicon Valley and raised $1.3 million in funding for their startup. Their story is inspiring because they followed a different path to success. Many young people dream of working in tech hubs like Silicon Valley where innovation and money meet. These teens show that you do not always need traditional routes like engineering degrees to succeed. Investors believe in their ideas and potential. This example encourages young learners to explore new opportunities and follow their passions. Their journey could inspire more students to think globally and creatively.
