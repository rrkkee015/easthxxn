## 1. Remembering 9/11: Families Gather to Honor the Day 

Today marks the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks. Families of those who lost loved ones gathered at the World Trade Center in New York, the Pentagon in Washington, and the crash site in Shanksville, Pennsylvania. The ceremonies are a time to **reflect on** the tragic events that changed America forever. Leaders and survivors **pay tribute to** the bravery of first responders and the spirit of unity that followed. This anniversary also reminds people why security and peace remain important. Many speeches **highlighted the legacy** of 9/11 and its impact on the country’s policies and communities. These moments help us **remember the past** while looking forward to a safer future.

## 2. How 9/11 Changed U.S. Immigration Policy Forever 

The attacks on September 11, 2001, led to major changes in U.S. immigration rules. The government created new laws to **strengthen border security** and prevent future attacks. These changes made immigration processes more strict and complicated for many people. Today, experts **reflect on** how these policies still affect immigrants and refugees. Some argue the rules are too tough and separate families. Others say security must remain a top priority. The anniversary is a chance to think about how immigration and safety can be balanced in the future.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **strengthen border security** — 국경 보안을 강화하다  
>   _After 9/11, the U.S. worked hard to **strengthen border security**._  
> - **reflect on** — ~에 대해 깊이 생각하다

> - **separate families** — 가족을 갈라놓다

## 3. U.S. Job Market Shows Strength Despite Inflation Concerns 

In August, the U.S. added 162,000 new jobs, showing the job market is still strong. This **hiring burst** surprises many economists because inflation is still high. More jobs usually mean people have money to spend, which can cause prices to rise more. Experts say this puts the government in a difficult position. They want to control inflation but also keep the economy growing. The increase in jobs means businesses still need workers, and many Americans are working again. The situation will be watched closely in the coming months to see if inflation slows down.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **hiring burst** — 갑작스러운 채용 증가  
>   _There was a **hiring burst** in August with many new jobs._  
> - **surprise economists** — 경제학자들을 놀라게 하다  
>   _The strong job numbers **surprise economists**._  
> - **put in a difficult position** — 곤란한 상황에 놓다  
>   _The data **puts the government in a difficult position** about inflation._  
> - **watched closely** — 면밀히 관찰하다  
>   _Experts will **watch the job market closely** for changes._

## 4. America’s Tech Scene Grows Beyond Silicon Valley 

Silicon Valley has been the heart of America’s technology industry for decades. But now, the tech scene is **expanding beyond** this region. Cities like Austin, Denver, and Atlanta are becoming new centers for tech companies and startups. This shift means more jobs and innovation spread across the country. Some reasons include lower costs and a better quality of life in these cities. Experts say this change will make the tech industry more diverse and competitive. It also means **winning back** tech talent and companies is a challenge for Silicon Valley.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **expand beyond** — ~을 넘어 확장하다  
>   _Tech companies are **expanding beyond** Silicon Valley._  
> - **winning back** — 다시 되찾다  
>   _Silicon Valley is trying to **win back** companies and workers._  
> - **spread across** — ~에 걸쳐 퍼지다  
>   _Innovation will **spread across** many U.S. cities._  
> - **quality of life** — 삶의 질  
>   _Many people move to cities with a better **quality of life**._
