## 1. Macy’s 100th Thanksgiving Parade Returns with Famous Guests 

The Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade is back after a delay, celebrating its 100th year. The event will look **mostly the same** as before, with colorful floats, marching bands, and large balloons. This year, special guests LL Cool J and Gloria Gaynor will join the parade. The Macy’s parade is a long-time American tradition that many families **look forward to** every year. It marks the start of the holiday season and brings joy to millions watching on TV or in person. After the postponement, many people are excited the parade is **back on track**. This event shows how traditions can continue even after challenges.

## 2. Supreme Court to Review Trump’s Immigration Policies 

The U.S. Supreme Court is preparing to examine important cases about immigration rules made during the Trump administration. These rules affected asylum, which is protection given to people who leave their country because of danger. Many advocates want the court to restore asylum rights that Trump’s policies had limited. The court’s decision could change how the U.S. handles immigration for years. This is an important moment because it affects thousands of immigrants and how the country treats them. Many organizations are urging the court to block some deportations they say are unfair and unsafe. The Supreme Court’s choices will shape immigration law in the near future.

> - **look forward to** — 기대하다, 고대하다  
>   _We **look forward to** the court’s decision next month._  
> - **restore** — 회복하다, 되돌리다  
>   _The government plans to **restore** old environmental rules._  
> - **urge** — 촉구하다, 강력히 권하다

> - **handle** — 다루다, 처리하다  
>   _The company will **handle** all customer complaints._  
> - **unfair and unsafe** — 부당하고 위험한

## 3. U.S. Job Market Shows Signs of Improvement 

The U.S. job market is showing positive signs as hiring picks up and unemployment rates fall. Many companies are adding more workers, especially in white-collar jobs like office and professional work. However, the total number of these jobs is still about 1.5 million less than before the pandemic. Experts say the recovery is good, but it might take more time to get back to normal levels. The job report also highlights changes in different industries and regions. These trends affect millions of Americans who are **looking for work** or thinking about their careers.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **pick up** — 증가하다, 좋아지다  
>   _Sales tend to **pick up** during the holiday season._  
> - **fall** — 떨어지다, 감소하다  
>   _Unemployment rates **fell** last month._  
> - **look for work** — 일자리를 찾다  
>   _After graduating, many young people **look for work**._  
> - **signs of improvement** — 개선의 징후  
>   _The economy shows **signs of improvement** this quarter._  
> - **still about** — 여전히 대략 ~  
>   _There are **still about** 10,000 seats available for the event._

## 4. Why More American Tech Startups Are Moving to Shenzhen, China 

Many American tech startups are choosing to move from Silicon Valley to Shenzhen, China. Shenzhen is known as a fast-growing city with strong technology and manufacturing industries. Startups find it easier to **scale their business** there because of lower costs and access to suppliers. This trend shows how the tech world is changing and becoming more global. Although Silicon Valley is still important, some startups believe Shenzhen offers better chances to grow quickly. This move might change the future of tech innovation and where new ideas develop.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **scale their business** — 사업을 확장하다  
>   _The company plans to **scale its business** to new countries._  
> - **fast-growing** — 빠르게 성장하는  
>   _Seoul is a **fast-growing** city with many new companies._  
> - **access to** — ~에 접근, 이용할 수 있는  
>   _Having **access to** good education is important._  
> - **tech innovation** — 기술 혁신  
>   _The conference focused on **tech innovation** in health care._  
> - **move from A to B** — A에서 B로 이동하다  
>   _Many people **move from** small towns **to** big cities._
