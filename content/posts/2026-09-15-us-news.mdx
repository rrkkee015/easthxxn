## 1. Supreme Court Blocks New Mail Ballot Restrictions for Midterms  


The U.S. Supreme Court recently **blocked** a set of new rules that would have made it harder to use mail ballots in the upcoming midterm elections. These rules were meant to **restrict** how ballots could be sent and counted by mail. Many voting rights groups said these changes could **limit** voting access for many people. The Court’s decision means voters can continue to use mail ballots with fewer problems. This is important because mail voting became popular during the pandemic and helps many people vote safely. The ruling shows the Court’s role in **stepping in** to protect voting rights when new laws might cause problems. Now, election officials will keep the current mail ballot rules for the next election.

> - **block** — 막다, 차단하다  
>   _The Supreme Court decided to **block** the new law._  
> - **restrict** — 제한하다  
>   _The new rules **restrict** how many ballots can be mailed._  
> - **limit** — 한정하다, 제한하다  
>   _These changes could **limit** voter access._  
> - **stepping in** — 개입하다, 나서다  
>   _The Court is **stepping in** to protect voting rights._

## 2. Trump Administration Faces Legal Setbacks on Immigration Rules  


The Trump administration recently **suffered** two important legal defeats related to its immigration policies. Courts **ruled against** rules that made it harder for immigrants to get green cards and other benefits. These rulings are a big **setback** because they stop the government from enforcing some strict immigration laws. Many states and cities joined lawsuits to **challenge** these policies, saying they unfairly hurt immigrants and communities. The legal battles show how immigration remains a hot and difficult topic in the U.S. These court decisions might **open the door** for immigrants to have better access to legal protections again. The government could try to change the rules once more, but for now, the courts have put limits on the Trump-era policies.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **suffered** — (손해·패배를) 겪다, 당하다  
>   _The administration **suffered** a big loss in court._  
> - **ruled against** — ~에 반대하는 판결을 내리다  
>   _The court **ruled against** the new immigration rules._  
> - **setback** — 좌절, 방해  
>   _This is a major **setback** for the policy._  
> - **challenge** — 도전하다, 이의를 제기하다  
>   _States **challenge** the government’s immigration plan._  
> - **open the door** — 가능성을 열다  
>   _This ruling may **open the door** for more immigrant rights._

## 3. U.S. Adds 162,000 Jobs in August, Showing a Strong Economy  


In August, the United States saw a **burst** of 162,000 new jobs, which is a good sign for the economy. This number shows that companies are still **hiring** workers despite concerns about inflation and higher costs. Many experts say this strong job growth could make the Federal Reserve think twice about raising interest rates too much. The labor market stays **sturdy**, meaning people can find jobs more easily than before. However, some worry that if jobs grow too fast, inflation might stay high. Overall, this job report gives hope that the economy is still doing well and can handle challenges ahead. It also means more people have money to spend, helping businesses grow.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **burst** — 갑작스러운 급증  
>   _There was a **burst** of new jobs in August._  
> - **hiring** — 고용하는, 직원 뽑는  
>   _Companies are still **hiring** many workers._  
> - **sturdy** — 견고한, 탄탄한  
>   _The job market remains **sturdy** despite inflation._

## 4. America’s Tech Hubs Are Expanding Beyond Silicon Valley  


The U.S. tech industry is changing. For many years, Silicon Valley was the top place for tech jobs and startups. But now, other cities are **emerging** as new tech hubs. Places like Austin, Miami, and Denver are growing fast and attracting tech companies and talent. This shift is **driven** by lower costs, more space, and good quality of life in these cities. As a result, Silicon Valley is **losing** some of its crown as the largest tech talent market. This trend may **open up** more opportunities for tech workers across the country. It also means the tech industry is becoming more spread out, which could help innovation grow in new regions.

> ### 영어 표현 학습  
> - **emerging** — 새로 등장하는  
>   _New tech hubs are **emerging** outside Silicon Valley._  
> - **driven** — ~에 의해 움직이는, 원동력이 되는  
>   _The change is **driven** by lower costs and space._  
> - **losing** — 잃다, 놓치다  
>   _Silicon Valley is **losing** its top position._  
> - **open up** — 기회를 열다  
>   _This trend may **open up** new jobs for tech workers._
