## 1. Supreme Court Blocks Missouri's GOP-Favored Map Again 

The Supreme Court has blocked Missouri’s new congressional map for the third time. The map was designed to favor the Republican Party, but the court said it was unfair. This decision is important because it affects how people’s votes count in elections. Fair maps help make sure political power matches voters’ choices. The court’s action shows it is working to keep elections balanced. This fight over voting maps might continue, and it could influence future elections. People are watching to see how state leaders will respond and redraw the maps.

> - **block** — 막다, 차단하다  
>   _The Supreme Court decided to **block** the unfair map._  
> - **favor** — ~에 유리하게 만들다  
>   _The map was drawn to **favor** one party._  
> - **unfair** — 불공평한  
>   _The court said the map was **unfair** to voters._  
> - **affect** — 영향을 주다  
>   _This decision will **affect** future elections._  
> - **watch** — 주시하다, 지켜보다  
>   _People **watch** how the state redraws the map._

## 2. Wisconsin’s Caregiver Shortage Worsens Due to Immigration Rules 

Wisconsin is facing a serious shortage of caregivers, and some leaders say federal immigration policies are making the problem worse. Caregivers help take care of elderly and disabled people. Many caregivers come from other countries, but stricter immigration rules mean fewer workers can enter the state. This shortage affects families who need help and the health care system. Leaders want the government to change the rules to allow more caregivers to work legally. If the situation doesn’t improve, more people may struggle to get the care they need.

> - **worsen** — 악화되다

> - **affect** — 영향을 주다  
>   _The shortage **affects** families and health care._  
> - **allow** — 허락하다, 허용하다

## 3. U.S. Job Market Shows Mixed Signals in Summer 2026 

In June, hiring was worse than expected, but the White House says the labor market is on an **upward trajectory**. This means jobs may improve soon. In August, however, there was a strong hiring burst, with 162,000 new jobs added. The unemployment rate is at 4.1%, showing the job market is fairly healthy. These changes matter because they affect the economy and inflation. When more people work, they spend more money, which can raise prices. The government and experts watch these numbers closely to decide on policies.

## 4. Tech Industry Pushes Back Against New H-1B Fee Proposal 

A big tech group is asking the Trump administration to drop a new $103,265 fee proposal for H-1B visas. H-1B visas allow skilled foreign workers to work in the U.S. The tech industry says this high fee would hurt Silicon Valley and other tech hubs by making it harder to hire talented workers from abroad. Many tech companies depend on foreign experts to grow and stay competitive. The group wants the government to consider how this fee could slow innovation and job creation. This issue is important because the tech sector plays a big role in the U.S. economy.

> ### 영어 표현 학습
> - **push back** — 반대하다, 저항하다  
>   _The tech group **pushed back** against the fee proposal._  
> - **proposal** — 제안  
>   _The government made a new fee **proposal**._  
> - **hurt** — 해치다, 손상시키다  
>   _The fee would **hurt** hiring skilled workers._  
> - **depend on** — ~에 의존하다  
>   _Tech companies **depend on** foreign experts._  
> - **slow down** — 늦추다  
>   _The fee could **slow down** innovation._
